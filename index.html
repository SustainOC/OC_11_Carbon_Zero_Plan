Where this stands today

Start with what Okanagan College has actually done, because it is better than the College tends to say. Emissions in 2025 were 1,477 tCO2e against a 2007 baseline of 1,904. That is 22.4 per cent below baseline, where British Columbia required 16 per cent by 2025, so the College is ahead of the line it is legally held to and not behind it. Carbon intensity has fallen 54 per cent since 2007 while floor area grew 69 per cent, which means the College has been getting steadily more efficient the whole time it has been getting bigger. It retired 1,447 tonnes through the provincial offset programme at a cost of $36,175, and under the Carbon Neutral Capital Program that money is meant to return to institutions like this one as capital for emissions reduction work.

That is a real record on Scope 1 and Scope 2, the direct fuel we burn and the electricity we buy. It is also about four per cent of the picture. Everything above, every tonne in the statutory filing, every tonne the offset cheque covers and every tonne the 2030 target is set against, is the part British Columbia requires Okanagan College to count. Commuting, purchased goods and services, the materials in our buildings, business air travel and waste are not in it, are not required to be in it, and have never been counted. Later in this brief they are sized for the first time, and no plausible version of that number is small.

The reason this matters beyond compliance is that the legislated ladder is not an ambitious target. It is roughly the minimum the science asks for. The Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change's Sixth Assessment finds that holding warming to 1.5C requires global emissions about 43 per cent below 2019 levels by 2030 and net zero carbon dioxide by around 2050, which is close to the shape of British Columbia's own ladder of 40 per cent by 2030 and 80 per cent by 2050. Meeting it is the floor. Carbon Zero exists to turn the uncounted part into a number Okanagan College can be held to, and the first decision is whether the College is willing to be.

The next eight months, on one line

Every date on this line falls between now and the end of May 2027. The last three are fixed by someone else and they collide: the November 2026 STARS submission is where criterion A either holds or does not, the commuting baseline is Okanagan College's first Scope 3 tonnage, and the May 2027 statutory filing is the first one this plan will have shaped. Note what is not on this line. Nothing in the statutory carbon report requires Okanagan College to report a single Scope 3 category, so the commuting work is voluntary, sits outside the compliance cycle, and proceeds only if the College decides it should.

The three scopes, and which ones Okanagan College counts

Greenhouse gas accounting is not improvised. The Greenhouse Gas Protocol, published at ghgprotocol.org, is the standard almost every institution in the world reports against, and it divides emissions into three scopes with fifteen numbered categories inside the third. British Columbia's Carbon Neutral Government programme requires the first two and a single line of the third. The standard asks for all of it.

Who owns the gap, and where the tonnes actually are

The first chart is the reason this impact area exists. It runs from Okanagan College's 2025 reported position to the 2030 legislated requirement, and it sizes each feeder module by what that module actually owns inside British Columbia's statutory boundary. The second and third bars are not proposals; they are the entire holdings of Supply Chain and Transportation. Set both to zero and 288 tonnes remain.

Underneath the gap is a sixteen year record that no statutory filing contains. Okanagan College's own trend workbook splits every year into natural gas, electricity, fleet and office paper. Both emissions series are carried, offsettable and reported, because they answer different questions and British Columbia has tracked target progress on the offsettable basis since 2023.

The carbon case

The complete institutional inventory, with an owner against every line

This is the artefact the impact area exists to produce. Every other module in this plan has reported its own slice; nobody has laid all of them out together. Four sources are measured and they total 1,446.6 tCO2e. One source inside the mandatory boundary has never been measured at all. Every Scope 3 category except commuting is unquantified, and on the one BC comparator that has looked, extended emissions ran seven times operational emissions in the same year. Most of Okanagan College's footprint is currently counted in categories rather than in tonnes, because tonnes do not exist for it.

The levers, re-ranked on carbon effect

Feasibility rank is the order Okanagan College's own capacity and calendar point to. Carbon rank is the order the carbon frame points to. Where the two disagree by more than one place, something in the current approach needs revisiting, and here they disagree in both directions at once.

How every activity is tagged

Each of the 46 activities in this plan carries one scope tag and one action tag, with no exceptions. Twenty seven are Measure, sixteen are Enable and three are Abate. That distribution is the inverse of Infrastructure's and it is deliberate: every tonne this module reports is abated by an activity belonging to one of the other three, so an Abate activity here would be the same reduction claimed twice.

The STARS position, verified against the 3.0.1 manual

OP 6, worth 16 points, is the home credit, and Carbon Zero owns OP 6.1 outright. OP 6.1 is different from the rest of the credit: it is a completeness checklist that scores having the inventory rather than having low emissions, and it is the one place in the whole rubric where this module's actual work is rewarded.

Three findings are worth having before the table. Criterion A is a hard prerequisite, in the manual's own words: "Criterion A must be met to earn points for indicators 6.2, 6.3, and 6.4." Okanagan College almost certainly satisfies it through its annual statutory filing, and 12.08 points depend on that continuing to be true. Okanagan College is already at 1.25 of 4 in OP 6.1, which had not previously been calculated, because the biogenic separation the College has reported for years has never been claimed. And there is no Not Applicable route anywhere in OP 6 or in PA 1, 2 and 3: the manual's three distinct exclusion devices all appear elsewhere and none appears here, so the Structure and Funding precedent that removed ten points from the denominator does not repeat. Two peer caveats apply throughout: published Douglas and Langara credit patterns are STARS 2.2 and do not map to 3.0.1, and of all twelve institutions swept only UNBC holds a current 3.x rating.

The target, stated three times

One outcome in tonnes, split between the Scope 1 and 2 ladder that is already law and a Scope 3 position that nobody requires, one lever in a unit AASHE scores rather than Okanagan College self assesses, and then a third for the residual. The third card is a deliberate departure from the two card pattern, recorded here rather than made quietly: the offset cheque is the only unavoidable carbon cost the College carries, it is written every year, and neither of the other two cards says anything about it.

Three options, differing in what we commit to beyond the law

Six years, activity by activity

Forty six activities, each with an identifier, a scope tag and an action tag. Full wording, accountable positions and timing bands live in the detailed plan. Labels here are deliberately short. Read the tag distribution at the head of each year: a year with no Measure activities cannot report, and in this module a year with Abate activities is the exception rather than the rule.

The decisions we need

Ten decisions. The first two are the ones the whole impact area turns on, and neither can be settled inside this module. None of them requires capital to be committed today, and three of them ask the executive to decide something before a number arrives rather than after.

Open questions, with owners

Sixteen questions. One of them, the size of Scope 3, now carries a scaled estimate rather than a blank, and the roadmap is built to retire it category by category. Five of them are about whether Okanagan College's own data says what it appears to say, which is itself the finding: the College holds three different provincial target trajectories, three floor area figures and three employee FTE figures, and the main Kelowna electricity meter spent a year billing less than half of what the campus actually used.
